eFigure 2. Comparison of Observed Effect Sizes to Effect Sizes Obtained From a Permutation
Analysis of Randomly Assigned “Placebo Album” Dates

Notes: Figure shows the results of a permutation analysis in which we selected 10 random “placebo
album release” days from 2017-2022 and estimated our baseline models (which adjust for federal
holidays, day of the week, week of the year, and year fixed effects) to derive the effect size of album
releases on the number of traffic-related deaths, repeated for 1000 iterations. Panel A shows the
distribution of effect sizes for randomly selected dates (all days of the week); Panel B shows the
analogous distribution for randomly selected Fridays, given that most album releases were on Fridays.
The vertical line in each panel shows our observed effect size on actual album release days. This
simulation produced an effect size greater than the observed value in only 14 of 1000 iterations for
random dates and 20 of 1000 iterations for random Fridays.

eFigure 3. Traffic Fatalities on Dates Matching Each Album’s Release Day in Other Study
Years

Notes: Figure shows the adjusted number of traffic fatalities on the same calendar date as each album’s
release date in all other study years (2017-2022), with each placebo date matched to the nearest weekday
of the original release. This falsification analysis isolates holiday-adjacent or seasonal travel patterns tied
to the calendar date itself by reproducing the temporal context of the release day in adjacent years without
the album release event. Estimates are from the same event-study specification as the primary analysis.
Error bars indicate 95% CIs.

eFigure 4. Traffic Fatalities on Album Release Fridays Compared with Fridays Before and After

Notes: Weekday-matched sensitivity analysis restricted to the albums released on Fridays, comparing
fatalities on each release Friday with fatalities on the Fridays immediately before and after (±7 days).
Panel A shows the incidence rate ratio of traffic fatalities by day relative to release; the release Friday
(day 0, red) had a rate ratio of 1.13 (95% CI, 1.05–1.21) versus its adjacent control Fridays (gray; dashed
line indicates a rate ratio of 1). Panel B shows the pooled daily fatalities underlying these ratios (release
Fridays, 144; adjacent control Fridays, ~127). Because there are two control Fridays and one release
Friday, exact binomial methods accounting for differing exposure time (2 control days vs 1 release day)
were used to estimate the rate ratio. Error bars indicate 95% CIs.

eFigure 5. Effect of Album Releases on Traffic Fatalities by Number of Albums Included

Notes: Each point shows the adjusted excess number of traffic fatalities on album release days relative to
the surrounding 10 days when the cohort is defined as the top 5, 10, 15, 20, or 25 albums ranked by first-
day Spotify streams, estimated with the same event-study model as the primary analysis. The effect was
statistically significant at every cohort size. Error bars indicate 95% CIs.

eFigure 6. Change in Traffic Fatalities on Each of the 10 Most-Streamed Album Release Days

Notes: Each point is the difference between traffic fatalities on an album's release day and the mean of the
6 same-weekday control days at 1, 2, and 3 weeks before and after release, excluding control days falling
on a public holiday or within 3 days of another album's release. Error bars are 95% CIs derived from the
standard error of the control-day mean. Albums are ordered by first-day streams (highest at top). The
overall estimate (diamond) is the mean of the 10 album-specific differences, consistent with the primary
analysis.
